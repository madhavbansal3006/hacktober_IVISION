## 4. Cross-dataset compatibility

- **CRS mismatch is real**: raster is in UTM 43N (EPSG:32643), both vector layers are in WGS84 (EPSG:4326). Any pipeline must explicitly reproject the incoming GPS point to EPSG:32643 before sampling the raster, and can do boundary/watershed containment checks directly in EPSG:4326. This is a standard operation, not a blocker — just noting it's not "already aligned" as one might assume.
- **Raster spatial coverage vs. boundary**: reprojecting the raster's bounding box to WGS84 and comparing to the boundary polygon shows the raster bbox contains the boundary polygon almost exactly (uncovered area is ~9×10⁻⁹ of the boundary area — floating-point/reprojection noise, not a real gap). So the raster fully covers the Shiggaon boundary.
- **Micro-watersheds vs. boundary**: the union of all 66 watershed polygons covers 100.00% of the boundary polygon's area, and all 66 watersheds intersect the boundary. Only 18 of 66 are *fully* contained inside the boundary — the rest extend beyond it, which is expected for watershed delineation (watersheds don't respect administrative boundaries). Implication for the pipeline: a point can be inside the Shiggaon boundary AND inside a micro-watershed that itself extends outside the boundary — that's fine, but a point could also fall inside a micro-watershed polygon while being outside the Shiggaon boundary. The order of operations in your spec (boundary check first, then watershed lookup) is correct and necessary — don't reverse it.
